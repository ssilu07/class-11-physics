What is Motion in a Plane?

When an object moves in two dimensions simultaneously, it is called motion in a plane. Unlike 1D motion, both x and y components must be tracked independently.

Key insight: A 2D motion can always be split into two independent 1D motions along perpendicular axes.
🎯
Projectile Motion
Motion under gravity with horizontal velocity — a parabolic path
🔄
Circular Motion
Constant speed, changing direction — centripetal acceleration always inward
↗️
Vectors
Displacement, velocity, acceleration — all have magnitude and direction
🚢
Relative Motion
Velocity of one object as seen from another moving frame

Projectile Motion

A projectile is launched with initial speed u at angle θ to the horizontal. Gravity acts only downward.

Horizontal (x) — No acceleration

xₜ = u·cos θ · t

Vertical (y) — Uniform acceleration g

yₜ = u·sin θ · t − ½g·t²

Key Results

QuantityFormula
Time of flightT = 2u·sin θ / g
Maximum rangeR = u²·sin 2θ / g
Max heightH = u²·sin²θ / 2g
Max range angleθ = 45°
Complementary angles (e.g. 30° and 60°) give the same range!

Uniform Circular Motion

Object moves on a circle of radius r with constant speed v but continuously changing direction.

Angular quantities

ω = 2π/T = 2πf    v = rω

Centripetal Acceleration

aᶜ = v²/r = ω²r    (directed toward center)

Centripetal Force

F = mv²/r = mω²r
Centripetal force is NOT a new force — it is provided by tension, gravity, normal force, friction, etc.

Period and Frequency

T = 2πr/v    f = 1/T    [f in Hz, T in s]

Relative Velocity in 2D

Velocity of object A as seen from object B:

v⃗_AB = v⃗_A − v⃗_B

River-boat problem

Boat velocity relative to ground = boat velocity relative to river + river velocity

v⃗_ground = v⃗_boat + v⃗_river

To cross river in minimum time

Point boat perpendicular to river bank

To cross river in minimum distance

Aim upstream at angle θ = sin⁻¹(v_river/v_boat)

Projectile Motion

Horizontal: x = u·cos θ · t
Vertical: y = u·sin θ · t − ½g·t²
Time of flight: T = 2u·sin θ / g
Range: R = u²·sin 2θ / g
Max height: H = u²·sin²θ / 2g
Velocity at t: vₓ = u·cos θ, vᵧ = u·sin θ − gt
Speed at t: v = √(vₓ²+vᵧ²)

Circular Motion

v = rω = 2πr/T = 2πrf
Centripetal accel: aᶜ = v²/r = rω²
Centripetal force: F = mv²/r
Angular velocity: ω = dθ/dt = 2π/T
Angular accel: α = dω/dt
Arc length: s = rθ

Vectors

1. A ball is thrown at 45° with speed 20 m/s. What is the maximum range? (g = 10 m/s²)

R = u²sin2θ/g = 400×1/10 = 40 m. At 45°, sin2θ = sin90° = 1.

2. In projectile motion, which component of velocity remains constant throughout?

Horizontal velocity (uₓ = u cos θ) is constant because no horizontal force acts (ignoring air resistance).

3. A car moves in a circle of radius 50 m at speed 10 m/s. Centripetal acceleration?

aᶜ = v²/r = 100/50 = 2 m/s². Always directed toward the center.

4. Two projectiles launched at angles 30° and 60° with the same speed. Their ranges are:

Complementary angles (30°+60°=90°) give identical range. R = u²sin2θ/g, and sin60°=sin120°.

5. Centripetal force in uniform circular motion does:

Centripetal force is perpendicular to velocity, so it does no work and only changes the direction of motion.

6. A boat can go 5 m/s in still water. River flows at 3 m/s. Minimum crossing time for 60 m width?

Minimum time = width/v_boat = 60/5 = 12 s. Point boat perpendicular to bank regardless of current.

7. At the highest point of projectile, velocity is:

At the peak, vertical component vᵧ = 0. Only horizontal component (u cos θ) remains.

8. Angular velocity ω of a body completing 5 revolutions per second?

ω = 2π × f = 2π × 5 = 10π ≈ 31.4 rad/s.

Quick Revision Notes

Projectile Motion — Key Points

  • Path is a parabola
  • Horizontal acceleration = 0; Vertical acceleration = g (downward)
  • At max height: vᵧ = 0, speed = u cos θ (minimum)
  • Angles 45° gives max range; 30° and 60° give same range
  • Time of flight proportional to u sin θ

Circular Motion — Key Points

  • Speed constant, velocity changes direction continuously
  • Centripetal acceleration always points toward center
  • Centripetal force ≠ a new force; it's provided by existing forces
  • No work done by centripetal force (F ⊥ v)
  • In vertical circle, speed is not uniform (gravity helps/opposes)

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up time of flight vs time to max height (T/2)
  • Thinking centripetal force is "outward" — it is INWARD
  • Forgetting that at 45° range is maximum (not 90°)
  • Confusing ω (rad/s) with f (rev/s); ω = 2πf

NCERT Important Concepts

Scalar: magnitude only (speed, distance, mass, temperature)
Vector: magnitude + direction (velocity, displacement, force)

Position Vector

r⃗ = xî + yĵ    magnitude |r⃗| = √(x²+y²)

Average vs Instantaneous

Average velocity = Δr⃗/Δt; Instantaneous = lim(Δt→0) Δr⃗/Δt = dr⃗/dt
