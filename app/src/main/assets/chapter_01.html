🌐 What is Physics?

Physics is the branch of science that deals with the study of matter, energy, and their interactions. It seeks to understand fundamental laws of nature through observation, experimentation, and mathematical formulation.

Scope of Physics

  • Macroscopic domain: Classical mechanics, thermodynamics — objects visible to the naked eye
  • Microscopic domain: Quantum mechanics — atoms, nuclei, electrons and subatomic particles
  • Mesoscopic domain: Between macro and micro — nanoscale systems (~10⁻⁹ m)
  • Cosmological domain: The Universe, galaxies, black holes (scales up to 10⁺²⁵ m)

⚖ Nature of Physical Laws

  • Physical laws are universal — valid everywhere in the universe at all times
  • They are symmetrical — laws don't change with time (time symmetry), position (space symmetry), or orientation (rotational symmetry)
  • They are based on observations and experiments that can be independently verified
  • A hypothesis becomes a theory and then a law after rigorous testing and verification
  • Conservation laws (energy, momentum, charge, angular momentum) are pillars of physics
Conservation of Energy: E_total = KE + PE = constant (in isolated system)
Conservation of Momentum: p_total = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = constant (no external force)
Conservation of Charge: Total charge in an isolated system always remains constant

⚡ The Four Fundamental Forces

All forces in nature can be traced back to just four fundamental interactions:

💥

Strong Nuclear

Binds protons & neutrons in nucleus. Range: ~10⁻¹⁵ m

☢

Weak Nuclear

Responsible for radioactive decay. Range: ~10⁻¹⁸ m

⚡

Electromagnetic

Between charged particles. Range: Infinite (1/r²)

🌐

Gravitational

Between any two masses. Range: Infinite (1/r²)

F_gravity = G·m₁·m₂/r² where G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
F_em = k·q₁·q₂/r² where k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²

💻 Physics & Technology Connection

  • Steam engine → Thermodynamics → Industrial Revolution
  • Maxwell's equations → Radio, television, wireless communication, internet
  • Quantum mechanics → Transistors, semiconductors, computers, smartphones
  • Nuclear physics → Nuclear power plants, medical imaging (X-rays, MRI, PET)
  • Laser physics → CDs, fiber optics, surgical lasers, barcode scanners
  • General relativity → GPS satellites need relativistic time corrections to work
