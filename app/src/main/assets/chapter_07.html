Rotational Motion — Core Concepts

Master spinning objects, angular quantities, and conservation laws.

Fundamental Concepts

Center of Mass

The point where the entire mass of a system can be considered concentrated. For a system of particles:

r_cm = (Sm·r) / M

Moment of Inertia

Rotational analog of mass — resistance to angular acceleration. Depends on mass distribution about the rotation axis.

I = S m·r²

Torque

Rotational analog of force. Causes angular acceleration. It is the cross product of the position vector and force.

t = r x F = I·a

Angular Momentum

Rotational analog of linear momentum. Conserved when the net external torque on the system is zero.

L = I·w = r x p

Angular Kinematics

Angular counterparts of linear equations. Uses angular acceleration a, angular velocity w, and angle q.

w = w0 + at | q = w0t + ½at²

Rolling Motion

Combination of translation and rotation. No-slip condition: v_cm = Rw. Total KE includes both types.

KE = ½Mv² + ½Iw²
Axis Theorems

Parallel Axis Theorem

Moment of inertia about any axis parallel to the CM axis, at distance d from it:

I = I_cm + M·d²

Perpendicular Axis Theorem

For a 2D lamina: sum of moments about two in-plane perpendicular axes equals moment about the normal axis.

I_z = I_x + I_y

Conservation of L

When net external torque = 0, angular momentum is conserved. A skater pulling arms in spins faster!

I1w1 = I2w2 (if t_ext = 0)

Rotational KE

Kinetic energy stored in rotational motion of a rigid body spinning about a fixed axis.

KE_rot = ½·I·w²
